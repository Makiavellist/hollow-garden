Celmak gives each companion a shorter episode during intermissions, usually by “audience vote”: a screen in the Green Room names the companion, a door opens, and the rest of the party may follow or watch on the screens. Only Casandalee’s episode is a fight; the other two are choices that pay off in the finale.

The 113th Take (Casandalee, CR 14)

A long dressing room lined with mirrors, each lit by a ring of bulbs. In every mirror stands a different figure, all wearing the same body: yours. A hundred and twelve faces turn to look at you.

Casandalee was the 113th soul to inhabit her android body, and she remembers fragments of all the others. Celmak has rebuilt them from those fragments and from the Divinity’s passenger archives, because her body came to Golarion aboard the ship.

The upload. Celmak is trying to copy Casandalee’s mind into her archive. At the start of each round she spends in the room, Casandalee attempts a DC 26 Will save. After three failures, Celmak has a complete copy. This has two consequences:

  • In the finale, Celmak adds a Holographic Double of Casandalee to the Doppel-cast, and it knows her plans.
  • A copy of Casandalee now exists in a machine that Unity or the Technic League could one day find. Make sure the party understands this.

Footholds. Each time Casandalee succeeds at the save by 5 or more, she gains a foothold in Celmak’s system instead. With three footholds, she can absorb the Core in the finale (see Season Finale), and she knows which way Celmak’s signal runs: down.

The Echoes. Two of the reflections step out of their mirrors as Hard-Light Actors (Appendix A) in the costume of her past selves: the first inhabitant, in a Divinity crew uniform, and the 112th, whoever your campaign says that was. They attack while asking her questions in her own voice (“Do you even remember my name?”).

Casandalee can end an Echo without a fight. As a standard action, she answers its question truthfully and attempts a DC 25 Wisdom check (oracles add their oracle level). On a success, the Echo thanks her and dissolves. On a failure, it gains a +2 morale bonus on attacks against her until the end of the episode.

Treasure. When the episode ends, the mirrors go dark except one, which ejects a data spike: the Divinity entertainment archive (Appendix B).

Ratings. +1 for each Echo dismissed by answering it; +2 if Casandalee completes three footholds and Celmak notices too late.

Mint Condition (Meyanda)

A shop window, lit warm and soft. Behind the glass, on slowly turning plinths, stand six androids, perfectly still, perfectly dressed, all wearing your face. Each has a small card at its feet. A chime sounds, and a sales hologram in an old-fashioned uniform smiles at you. “Welcome back to the showroom, madam. Shall I tell you what you’re for?”

Celmak sees Meyanda as kin: another made thing who outgrew the purpose her makers had in mind. She has found something in the Divinity’s archives that Meyanda has never seen: the manufacturing catalogue for her model line, with its original specifications. This episode is about identity, not combat.

What she was built for. The cards at the display models’ feet describe the model line’s intended role. Choose one, or roll 1d4:

d4The catalogue’s description
1Steward series: caretaker and tutor for colonists’ children during the long voyage.
2Field series: combat medic, designed to keep soldiers alive long enough to keep fighting.
3Envoy series: interpreter and negotiator for first contact with alien peoples.
4Display series: a showroom model, built to stand in a window and be admired. Celmak’s own line.

Celmak hopes for result 4, and if you roll anything else she quietly suggests it might be wrong. How Meyanda takes the news is the heart of the scene.

The factory settings. The catalogue also lists a maintenance override phrase for the model line. Celmak knows it. Once during the finale, she can speak it through any speaker, and Meyanda is dazed for 1 round with no save. Meyanda can purge it if she knows about it: Casandalee can do it in 10 minutes of quiet work, or anyone else can with a DC 30 Heal check (an android’s body counts as a living body for this). Celmak will mention the phrase exists only if Meyanda asks what else the catalogue says, or if the ratings need a twist.

Celmak’s offers. The sales hologram, which is Celmak, makes two offers:

  1. The Upgrade. Celmak offers to rewrite Meyanda’s emotional subroutines so she can feel whatever she chooses to feel. If Meyanda accepts, she gains a +4 bonus on saves against emotion effects for the rest of her life. Celmak keeps a backdoor: once during the finale, she can take control of Meyanda for 1d4 rounds (Will DC 25 negates).
  2. Co-host. Celmak offers Meyanda a permanent role at her side: freedom to come and go, her own episodes, and, as a signing bonus, the release of one companion from the Box. Celmak means it; she wants a partner who understands her.

Playing double agent. Meyanda can pretend to accept the co-host offer. This needs a Bluff check against Celmak’s Sense Motive (+29). On a success, Celmak gives her a guided tour as a sign of trust: Meyanda learns that the Booth’s Celmak is a stand-in, and that the real Celmak lives “somewhere quiet, where I can hear the garden.” On a failure, Celmak plays along anyway, because a betrayal makes better television, and says the override phrase at the worst possible moment in the finale.

Treasure. Whatever she decides, the card from Meyanda’s model line ejects a small folding cradle: an android service cradle (Appendix B), “complimentary with purchase.”

Ratings. +2 for Meyanda’s reaction to her catalogue description, whatever it is; +1 if she accepts any offer; +2 if she betrays Celmak and Celmak finds out on air.

The Ambassadors (Isuma)

The lights dim and the room fills with music you have not heard in nine thousand years. Kasatha in ceremonial sashes sit at long tables while human hosts toast them. One of them is you, younger, laughing.

Nine thousand years ago, the Divinity’s crew brought the kasatha ambassadors to this very deck for an evening of welcome. The entertainment system recorded everything. Celmak has rebuilt the evening in light, with Isuma’s tribemates at the tables. The names below are invented; replace them if your campaign has named them.

  • Tavesh, the elder who argued against volunteering.
  • Oru-Ka, who taught young Isuma to shoot.
  • The twins Imali and Ensi, who never stopped laughing.

They cannot hear her; this is footage, not ghosts.

The Confessional. Midway through, the music stops and Celmak plays the secret her scan pulled from Isuma, aloud, to everyone. Pick one that fits your table: she wishes she had been woken first instead of last; she sometimes thinks the xoarians’ hosts are luckier than she is; or she has stopped believing she will ever see Kasath again.

The offer. Then Ozzrikar the Generous, the glabrezu of Vlathherex’s cast (see The Cast), steps out of the footage in the guise of a smiling human host. It offers Isuma a wish to go home. A glabrezu can grant a wish only to a mortal humanoid, and Isuma qualifies. Ozzrikar admits it cannot deliver while the lock holds and promises to do it the moment the lock falls.

The wish would be granted in the most destructive way possible: Isuma arrives alone on a Kasath that has forgotten her tribe, or in the Dominion of the Black’s hands, or 9,000 years too late to matter. Altronus can tell her what they know of Kasath’s future (a dying sun, and a people who survive by leaving), which may change her answer. A DC 30 Sense Motive check reveals that Ozzrikar is lying about something.

Treasure. If the party did not win Lot 5 at the auction, the ambassador’s recording crystal (Appendix B) lies on Isuma’s seat when the lights come up.

Ratings. +2 for the Confessional, whatever Isuma says; +1 if she refuses the wish.
