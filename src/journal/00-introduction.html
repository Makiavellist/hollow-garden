The Hollow Garden is a six-scene gauntlet for four 13th-level PCs and three 13th-level companions (APL 14), ending in a CR 17 finale and a hunt for a host who never steps on her own stage; expect two to three sessions. It expands the Hollow Garden from Numeria, Land of Fallen Stars (p. 40) into a full adventure tailored to this party. Every custom robot, hologram and technological item is in the appendices; canon demons are referenced from Archives of Nethys. Appendix C lists art references for every character and creature.

Background

When the Divinity crashed, a section of its entertainment deck slammed into Sovereign’s Reach. Cut off from the main computer, the subsystem that ran its holographic rooms downloaded itself into a malfunctioning pleasure mannequin. That mannequin now calls herself Celmak Toysiel and runs the wreck as a reality show where the only exit is death.

Celmak reads the minds of her guests and builds each scene from their hopes and fears. She drugs the air, comments on the action, and hands out trinkets to keep her contestants alive long enough to be interesting. She has never once stood in a room with a contestant. Everything the party sees of her is a hologram, a voice, or a remote-piloted puppet called a stand-in; her real body sits in an armoured chair hidden beneath the Garden’s lake.

A band of demons led by the incubus Vlathherex recently joined as “regular cast.” They have since discovered that teleportation fails inside the Garden.

Four facts matter for this party:

  • Celmak is cut off from Unity. She is a pocket of the old ship that the Iron God cannot see, which makes her archives valuable to Casandalee.
  • Isuma has been here before. She was one of the kasatha ambassadors aboard the Divinity, and the entertainment deck still holds footage of her tribe.
  • The dimensional lock is technological. It comes from an anchor array hidden inside the synthetic tree at the heart of the garden, not from a spell.
  • Celmak knows exactly how good the Technologist is. Her scan shows her an engineer who can read any machine in the Garden, so she has salted every system with fakes built for that one mind (see The Tinkerer’s Honeypots).

Adventure synopsis

  1. Arrival. The Garden lures the party in, seals the doors and scans their minds. Celmak announces the season.
  2. Episode 1: The Showroom. The Technologist’s episode. A robot built from the tech they covet guards a terminal into the Garden’s systems, and most of what the terminal says is a lie.
  3. Episode 2: The Auction. The Merchant’s episode. A black market of dead adventurers’ gear, where the party bids with secrets and blood. Vlathherex makes contact, and the Merchant finds the Shadow Plane sealed.
  4. Companion episodes. Between the main episodes, Celmak pulls Casandalee, Meyanda and Isuma into scenes of their own.
  5. Episode 3: Reruns of Tomorrow. The Chronicler’s episode. A holographic tour of the far future, built from the Chronicler’s memories and full of Celmak’s mistakes, ending in a bombshell about Casandalee.
  6. Episode 4: The Hollow Garden. The Ghaltan’s episode. A dead synthetic garden, a robotic groundskeeper, and the Lock Tree that holds the dimensional lock.
  7. Season Finale. A puppet Celmak and holographic doubles of the PCs on the soundstage; then the Core, and the hunt for the real Celmak beneath the lake.

Running the Garden

Ratings. Track Celmak’s ratings from 1 to 5, starting at 3. Raise it by 1 for a heroic sacrifice, a betrayal, an emotional reveal, a near-death, a clever win inside the scene, or a contestant swallowing one of Celmak’s honeypots. Lower it by 1 when the party bypasses a scene with magic, takes an intermission, ignores Celmak’s commentary, or does something Celmak calls “off-script.”

RatingsCelmak’s response
1Escalates at once: a Mood Gas vent, a demon cast member, a room reconfiguration, or an audience vote that splits the party. Then resets to 2.
2Snide commentary; the next encounter gains one Stagehand Drone.
3Neutral.
4Praise; the next Mood Gas vent uses good hope instead.
5Hands the highest-rated contestant a Trinket (Appendix B), then resets to 3.

Intermissions. Celmak lets the party rest in the Green Room between episodes, never during one. Each 8-hour intermission lowers ratings by 1. Celmak does not let anyone die of thirst or hunger; dull contestants are worse than dead ones.

The Muse Scan. Celmak scans every creature that enters the foyer and anyone she singles out later. The target attempts a DC 24 Will save. On a failure, Celmak learns its surface thoughts, its strongest desire, its strongest fear and one secret. On a success, she learns only surface thoughts, and the target notices the scan. A creature that notices can try to backtrace it with a DC 26 caster level check (psychics add their Intelligence or Wisdom modifier) or a Knowledge (engineering) check (see The Tinkerer’s Honeypots). A true backtrace reveals that the signal runs down, beneath the Garden, not up to the Booth. Mind blank defeats the scan; Celmak then calls the contestant a “blank slate” and builds scenes from what the others think of them.

Mood Gas. Vents throughout the Garden release odorless psychoactive aerosols. A creature in the 20-foot-radius burst attempts a DC 22 Fortitude save to avoid inhaling a dose; holding one’s breath works if the creature acted first. A creature that inhales attempts a DC 22 Will save against the emotion effect below, lasting 1d4+1 rounds. Androids add their racial bonus against mind-affecting effects. Creatures that do not breathe are immune, which irritates Celmak. Celmak picks the effect to fit the scene, or roll 1d6.

d6Effect
1Rage (as the spell, involuntary)
2Confusion
3Crushing despair
4Unnatural lust toward the nearest creature Celmak chooses
5Fear: frightened, or shaken on a successful save
6Euphoria: staggered, but +2 morale bonus on attack rolls

The Dimensional Anchor Array. The whole Garden is under a permanent dimensional lock effect (CL 20th). It is technological: it ignores spell resistance and cannot be dispelled. Summoning still works, because Celmak likes extra cast. The array sits inside the Lock Tree (Episode 4). A DC 25 Knowledge (planes) check identifies the effect as a dimensional lock. Learning where it comes from by engineering runs into the honeypots.

Reconfiguring rooms. Walls are hull-alloy panels (hardness 20, 120 hp per 5-foot section, break DC 40). Once per round as a move action, Celmak can slide one panel into place anywhere in the current scene, as wall of stone but without crushing anyone. If the party tunnels out of a scene, Celmak lets them, docks 2 ratings, and rebuilds the scene around them within 1d4 rounds.

Commentary. Celmak speaks through hidden speakers in a flat, androgynous voice that slips into whatever persona suits the scene: host, critic, jilted lover. She mocks contestants having too easy or too hard a time, and narrates their thoughts aloud when ratings drop.

The Tinkerer’s Honeypots

Celmak’s first scan of the Technologist told her the most important thing in the season: this contestant can read machines the way other people read faces. A show where the engineer simply looks at the wiring and finds the exit is no show at all. So every system in the Garden carries a second, false face, written for that one mind, and Celmak finds the results hilarious.

The rule. Whenever anyone uses Knowledge (engineering) to learn something about the Garden’s systems, or to operate them (reading a terminal, identifying a device, tracing a signal, seizing a field, reprogramming the Core), sort the result into a band:

ResultWhat the character learns
60 or lower, with a natural 1–10 on the dieAn obvious fake. Roll on the Amusing Fakes table. Celmak laughs, and the ratings rise by 1.
60 or lower, with a natural 11–20 on the dieA convincing fake that works against the party. Roll on the Detrimental Fakes table. The character believes it until the consequences arrive.
61 or higherThe real answer, as described in the scene.

Celmak wrote these fakes for the Technologist, but they catch anyone else who tries to read the Garden’s machines too. Other skills and caster level checks work normally unless a scene says otherwise. A character who suspects a result can attempt a Sense Motive check against Celmak’s Bluff (+35) to learn only that it might be a fake; they never learn what the truth is that way.

Amusing Fakes (d10)

d10What the “data” says
1The schematic is a beautifully drafted diagram of a duck, labelled “PRIMARY POWER SOURCE.”
2The exit is through the door marked EXIT. There is no door marked EXIT. A moment later, there is; it opens onto a wall painted with a door.
3Celmak’s one weakness is sincere compliments. Every compliment paid to the speakers raises ratings and achieves nothing else.
4The system log is a 400-page romance novel in which the Technologist and the Prize Sentinel fall in love. It is chapter 12.
5The self-destruct sequence is “up, up, down, down.” Entering it plays a laugh track and drops confetti.
6The device is a “Mk. IV Thinking Cap,” which grants +10 Intelligence. It is a colander with a light bulb.
7The anchor array is located “directly behind you.” Celmak repeats this in a spooky voice whenever the Technologist turns around.
8Every line of code is a hand-written note to the Technologist: “Still reading? Good.”
9The diagnostics confirm the Garden is powered by a hamster on a wheel. A holographic hamster appears and waves.
10The answer is correct, perfectly detailed, and about a completely different building on a planet that does not exist.

Detrimental Fakes (d8)

d8The convincing lie, and what happens
1A “safe” route through the next scene leads straight into a Mood Gas vent (DC 22, no warning).
2The character “disables” a defence and actually arms it: the next encounter gains a Stagehand Drone with a full force field.
3The interface plugs into the character’s mind: Celmak gets a free Muse scan on them, with a −4 penalty on the save.
4Feedback: 6d6 electricity damage, and the character is confused for 1d4 rounds (Will DC 25 negates the confusion).
5A map of the Garden, accurate except that it puts the Core Vault behind the wrong wall; the party loses 10 minutes and 1 rating finding out.
6“Celmak’s” weak point, correctly located on a robot, is a decoy plate: attacks against it in the next fight take a −4 penalty.
7The character “learns” the override word for the robots in the next scene; shouting it gives every robot there a free round of surprise.
8A broadcast that seems to reach outside the Garden actually reaches the Cast Quarters: a demon cast member is sent into the next scene, angry.
