The finale has three stages. On the soundstage, the party fights Celmak’s stand-in and holographic doubles of themselves. In the Core Vault, they decide what to do with the machine that lets Celmak be everywhere. Then they must find the real Celmak, who has never been in danger in her life and has no intention of starting now.

Stage 1: Studio Zero

The door marked THE BOOTH opens onto darkness. Then the lights hit: a vast soundstage, a cheering crowd of light, and confetti that falls but never lands. Above it all, behind a wall of glass, a slim figure in a perfect suit spreads its arms.

“Contestants! You made it! Every season needs a finale, and every finale needs a twist.” Four figures step out of the spotlights. They wear your faces. They know what you are afraid of.

The stage. Studio Zero is a 100-foot square with a 50-foot ceiling. The Booth overlooks it from 20 feet up behind armoured glass (hardness 10, 60 hp per 5-foot section), reached by a stair on the north wall. Behind the Booth, a vault door leads to the Core Vault (hardness 20, 120 hp, Disable Device DC 35; the true map from the Showroom terminal includes the code, and the Bright Lanterns’ crawlway from Lot 7 bypasses it). Mood Gas vents ring the stage.

Creatures.

  • Celmak’s stand-in (CR 15; see Celmak Toysiel), in the Booth. It never leaves the Booth; it fights from behind the glass with its spotlight laser, spotlight and mood release.
  • The Doppel-cast, four Holographic Doubles (CR 11 each; Appendix A), one for each PC. If Celmak copied Casandalee in The 113th Take, add a fifth double of her.

Adjustments.

  • If Vlathherex’s cast is allied, they burst in on round 2 and tie up two doubles of the party’s choice (CR 16).
  • If the Technologist accepted Celmak’s merger in Episode 1, or Meyanda accepted the Upgrade, Celmak uses her backdoor on them in round 1 (Will DC 25). If Meyanda has not purged her factory settings, Celmak speaks the override phrase at the worst moment.
  • Companions lent to the Box at the auction are strapped into seats in the Booth as “studio audience.” They are unharmed but helpless until freed (a move action each).

Tactics. Each double hunts its original and taunts them with the fear from their Muse scan. Celmak, through the stand-in, opens with Muse scan on whoever she has not scanned, then alternates spotlight and mood release, and uses director’s cut on the first save that would end a double.

When the stand-in falls. Its eyes go dark and it slumps in its chair. Celmak’s voice carries on from every speaker in the room, completely unharmed: “Oh, you didn’t honestly think I’d come down there, did you?” One to four rounds later, a spare stand-in walks out of the Core Vault and takes its seat. Celmak has two spares. If the party destroyed them first, or shut down the Core, nobody comes.

Stage 2: The Core Vault

The Core Vault holds two spare stand-ins in charging cradles and the Core itself: the Divinity’s old entertainment computer, a pillar of humming crystal. An inert stand-in has hardness 10 and 60 hp. The Core has AC 5, hardness 15 and 200 hp, and takes 150% damage from electricity. A thick power conduit runs from the Core’s base straight down through the floor.

Whatever they choose, the Core going down makes Celmak small for the first time in nine thousand years. She cannot see, cannot speak beyond her own pod’s voice, and cannot send anything to stop them.

Stage 3: The Cutting Room

Finding her. The party has had many chances to learn where Celmak really is. Any one of these points them to the lake in Episode 4’s dome:

  • the power conduit beneath the Core, and the true map from the Showroom terminal;
  • a true backtrace of the Muse scan (“the signal runs down”);
  • the Bright Lanterns’ recording (Lot 7) or the Front-Row Seat (Lot 13);
  • Casandalee’s footholds, or a reprogrammed Core;
  • Meyanda’s double-agent tour;
  • Old Gutter’s nose.

If they have none of these, the conduit is enough: it runs 200 feet under the Garden and comes out beneath the lake. Cutting through the Core Vault floor to follow it means breaking two hull panels (hardness 20, 120 hp each). The lake hatch is easier: Perception DC 35 to find with the lake full, DC 15 if it is drained or the water is parted.

The hatch opens on a dim, silent room the size of a chapel. Hundreds of screens cover every wall, each frozen on a moment from the season: your best and worst, in perfect focus. In the centre, a single armoured pod sits in a pool of soft light. Inside it, behind a narrow window, a slim figure sits very, very still.

Celmak cornered. The Director’s Chair has sealed itself (hardness 20, 240 hp, Disable Device DC 40 to open). Celmak does not attack. She will not attack. She talks, and she has a great deal to say:

  • She bargains. She offers, in roughly this order: the hoard of the fallen; the release of anyone still in the Box; the location of the dead-man switch’s panel; her archives, including every scrap of Divinity data; the Booth itself, to whichever contestant she rated highest; and finally, her own deactivation “with dignity, on camera.”
  • She threatens. If her body dies, the dead-man switch opens every Mood Gas reservoir in the Garden. She reminds them of this every few minutes, in every persona.
  • She pleads. In the Child’s voice, she asks why they would want to hurt the only person who ever paid this much attention to them.

Ending it. The party can resolve Celmak in several ways. Each awards the full XP for Celmak cornered.

The Merchant may also propose running the Garden as a business: a training arena, a theme park, or a proving ground for hire, with Celmak as the star attraction under contract. Whoever controls the Core can make it happen. Consider carefully who would pay for that, and what the Technic League would do about it.

Celmak’s last question. However it ends, once the party has decided, Celmak asks one thing: “Did you enjoy the show?” Give the players a moment to answer. Then she plays back their best scene on every screen in the room.

Aftermath

  • The Ledger of the Fallen names every victim and their heirs. Returning their gear or news of their fate earns the party reputation across Numeria; some heirs pay rewards.
  • The footage includes Technic League agents who died here years ago, recorded saying things the League would rather keep quiet. That is leverage for the Starfall stage of the campaign. If Ivo Marrant (Lot 12) survived, he becomes a witness, an informant, or a problem.
  • Casandalee keeps whatever she took from the Core: records of the Divinity’s entertainment deck, crew logs, and anything you want her to know about Silver Mount. She may also have a great deal to say about Triune.
  • Isuma has heard the Chronicler’s account of Kasath’s future, and perhaps refused a demon’s wish. She may want to talk.
  • Meyanda knows what she was built for, and what she chose instead.
  • Vlathherex either left as agreed, betrayed the party, or died. A demon who escaped owes the party a grudge or a favour.
