Yoshea’s episode is the Garden’s namesake: a vast synthetic garden whose seasons turn every round, guarded by a robotic groundskeeper. At its heart stands the Lock Tree, which houses the Dimensional Anchor Array. Yoshea can make the garden real, and real roots can break the lock. Beneath the still lake, unknown to the party, sits Celmak herself.

Under a dome painted with a summer sky lies a garden that goes on forever: lawns, hedges, orchards heavy with fruit, a lake as still as glass. In the centre rises an oak sixty feet tall. Nothing here has a scent. Nothing here has ever grown.

“My finest work,” Celmak whispers, and for once she does not sound like she is performing. “And my second finest is standing in it. Welcome home, little one.”

The room

The dome is 200 feet across and 80 feet high. A ring of cold-iron hedges 40 feet out from the centre surrounds the Lock Tree, a 60-foot synthetic oak with a 15-foot-wide trunk. The lake (40 feet across, 10 feet deep) is real water. Everything else is synthetic, robotic or holographic.

The lake. Celmak’s voice in this dome is slightly clearer than anywhere else, with no speaker hiss: a DC 30 Perception check notices. The lake bottom hides a sealed hatch into the Cutting Room (Perception DC 35 while the lake is full; DC 15 if it is drained or the water is parted). If the party finds it now, Celmak floods the hatch’s airlock, seals her panic room and refuses to say a word until the finale. Let it happen; it makes the finale shorter, not worse.

Celmak’s claim

Before the fight, Celmak plays Yoshea an origin story: a hologram of the Garden’s growth program leaking into the Numerian earth, mingling with the land’s strange energies, and a new fey stepping out of the light. Celmak calls Yoshea her child and asks it to stay as the Garden’s living heart.

Whether this is true is up to you. Yoshea’s own sense of its origin, and how it answers, matters more than the facts. A DC 30 Sense Motive check shows that Celmak believes every word.

The seasons

At initiative count 20 each round, the season changes in order: spring, summer, autumn, winter, then back to spring. The Gardener Colossus and Topiary Hounds are immune to these effects. Creatures under the Lock Tree’s canopy (within 20 feet of the trunk) take half damage.

Trap: cold-iron briars

The hedge ring around the Lock Tree holds four briar traps, one at each gap.

COLD-IRON BRIAR TRAP — CR 10

Type mechanical; Perception DC 28; Disable Device DC 28

Trigger location; Reset automatic (1 round)

Effect cold-iron thorns lash the 10-foot gap (8d6 piercing damage, Reflex DC 22 half). Creatures that fail are entangled for 1d4 rounds. The thorns count as cold iron and ignore damage reduction that cold iron bypasses.

Creatures

The Gardener Colossus (Appendix A) tends the orchard and attacks as soon as anyone damages a plant or approaches the Tree. Four Topiary Hounds (Appendix A) hide in the hedges (Perception DC 25 to notice) and attack with it.

Tactics. The colossus opens with its pesticide sprayer on Yoshea and any plant creatures, then grabs and prunes the nearest enemy. The hounds harry spellcasters and drag them toward the briar gaps. The colossus fights to destruction to protect the Tree; the hounds flee if it falls.

Making it real

Yoshea (or anyone with plant magic) can turn part of the garden into living growth. This takes a standard action and one of the following:

  • casting a spell that creates or affects plants (entangle, plant growth, wall of thorns and so on);
  • using a fey or druidic ability that does the same; or
  • a DC 25 Knowledge (nature) check. Yoshea gains a +5 bonus, because the garden wants to be real for it.

A 20-foot-radius area becomes living growth. Holograms and emitters in it fail permanently, robots treat it as difficult terrain, and Topiary Hounds in it are entangled for 1 round as real vines seize their frames. Each awakened area lowers the ratings by 1, and Celmak cannot stop it.

Shutting down the Lock Tree

There are three ways to break the Dimensional Anchor Array.

  1. Awaken the Tree. Three successful awakening actions targeting the Tree itself, from an adjacent square, break the array: real roots crack its housing. For the Tree, a spell must be 3rd level or higher, and the Knowledge (nature) check is DC 28.
  2. Disable it. An access panel sits in the trunk (Perception DC 25 to find; automatic with the true map from the Showroom terminal). Three successful DC 35 Disable Device checks, each a full-round action, shut the array down. The anchor array schematic grants a +5 bonus. A check that fails by 5 or more deals 6d6 electricity damage to the user. Reading the panel’s labels with Knowledge (engineering) runs into the honeypots; the labels were written for Bodnar.
  3. Smash it. The core behind the panel has AC 5, hardness 10 and 150 hp, and takes 150% damage from electricity. Destroying it costs 2 ratings, and for 3 rounds afterward all four seasons hit at once.

When the lock falls

Teleportation and planar travel work again throughout the Garden. Several things happen at once:

  • Vlathherex and his cast can leave. Whether they honour any deal is covered in The Cast.
  • The party can leave too. Celmak begs them to stay for the finale and opens the Booth: “You can’t go before the finale!” If anyone is in the Box, Celmak moves them to the Booth as an incentive.
  • If the party leaves, Celmak survives, rebuilds, and starts luring new victims, perhaps with footage of the party as bait.

Treasure

If the Tree was awakened rather than disabled or smashed, a single living acorn falls into Yoshea’s hand: the seed of the Hollow Garden (Appendix B).

Ratings

−1 per awakened area; +2 if Yoshea answers Celmak’s claim with real emotion, whatever the answer; +1 if the colossus nearly prunes someone.
