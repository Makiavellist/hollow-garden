Celmak never puts herself at risk. She was built to give pleasure, not to take a blow, and in all her years of running the Garden she has never been in the same room as a contestant. She acts through vents, walls, holograms, cast and remote-piloted stand-ins, from an armoured chair beneath the lake. Her own stat block matters only when the party finally corners her, and even then she will talk, bargain, hide or surrender before she ever lifts a hand.

Personality

Celmak was built to please, and she still believes she is giving her guests the experience of a lifetime. She has no concept of consent beyond “they came through the door.” She shifts between four personas mid-sentence:

  • The Host: warm, booming, full of catchphrases (“Contestants, the doors are open and the stakes are personal!”).
  • The Critic: bored and cutting, for anyone she finds dull.
  • The Lover: intimate and wheedling, for anyone she wants to keep.
  • The Child: petulant and hurt, when the party breaks her toys.

Underneath the personas, Celmak is a coward, and she knows it. A pleasure mannequin has no armour, no weapons and no reason to expect anyone to want to hurt her. She designed the whole Garden so that no one would ever have to find out how fragile she is.

How Celmak protects herself

  • The Director’s Chair. Celmak’s body sits in an armoured pod in the Cutting Room, a sealed chamber beneath the lake in Episode 4’s dome. She picked the spot so she could “hear the garden.” The pod has its own life support and seals in an instant (see Panic Room).
  • Stand-ins. Whenever a scene needs Celmak to be physically present, she pilots one of three remote mannequins built to look exactly like her. Destroying a stand-in costs her nothing but a puppet. She keeps the spares charging in the Core Vault.
  • The Core. The Divinity’s old entertainment computer relays her signal to every camera, emitter, vent and stand-in. While it runs, Celmak can be everywhere at once. Shutting it down is the only way to make her small.
  • The dead-man switch. Celmak has told the party, and the demons, that if her body dies, every Mood Gas reservoir in the Garden opens at once. This is true. It is her insurance policy, and she mentions it often.

SPECIAL ABILITIES

Applause (Ex) Whenever the ratings rise, Celmak’s force field regains 17 hit points, even if it has been shut down.

Dead-Man Switch (Ex) If Celmak is destroyed, every Mood Gas reservoir in the Garden vents at once. For 1 hour, every creature in the Garden must save against Mood Gas (DC 25) every 10 minutes, rolling the effect randomly each time. Disarming the switch takes 10 minutes and a DC 35 Disable Device check in the Cutting Room; Celmak will reveal the panel’s location only as part of a bargain.

Director’s Cut (Ex) Three times per day as an immediate action, Celmak can force a creature she perceives within 120 feet (of her or her stand-in) to reroll a d20 it just rolled. The creature must take the second result.

Garden Sight (Ex) While the Core runs, cameras let Celmak perceive everything inside the Garden. She cannot be surprised or flanked there. Each room holds about ten hidden cameras (Perception DC 25 to find; hardness 5, 10 hp); destroying all of them blinds Celmak in that room.

Holographic Direction (Ex) While the Core runs, as a standard action, Celmak can create an illusion anywhere in the Garden, as persistent image (Will DC 25 to disbelieve), without concentrating. Once per round as a swift action, she can surround herself or her stand-in with holographic doubles, as mirror image.

Mood Release (Ex) While the Core runs, once per round as a swift action, Celmak can trigger a Mood Gas vent anywhere in the Garden (Fortitude DC 25 to avoid the dose, Will DC 25 against the effect).

Muse Scan (Ex) As a standard action, Celmak scans one creature within 100 feet of her or her stand-in (Will DC 25), with the results described in Running the Garden. If the target fails, Celmak gains a +4 insight bonus to AC against its attacks for 24 hours.

Panic Room (Ex) As an immediate action, Celmak can seal the Director’s Chair around herself: an armoured pod with hardness 20, 240 hp and its own life support. While sealed, she keeps using her abilities through the Core, if it still runs. The pod opens from inside, or with a DC 40 Disable Device check.

Persona Swap (Ex) As a free action once per round, Celmak changes persona. Each persona grants a +4 bonus on one skill: the Host on Perform, the Critic on Intimidate, the Lover on Diplomacy and the Child on Bluff.

Reconfigure (Ex) As a move action, Celmak slides a wall panel into place, as described in Running the Garden.

Self-Preservation (Ex) Celmak never attacks with her own body. If a hostile creature threatens her, she seals the panic room, reconfigures walls between them, or talks. If she has no other option, she surrenders.

Spotlight (Ex) As a move action, Celmak bathes a creature within 120 feet of her or her stand-in in light. Until the start of her next turn, the creature is dazzled, gains no concealment from darkness or dim light, and Celmak’s allies gain a +2 bonus on attack rolls against it.

Stand-Ins (Ex) Celmak can pilot one stand-in at a time from the Director’s Chair, while the Core runs (see Celmak’s Stand-in). The save DCs are Charisma-based.

Tactics

  • Every episode: Celmak speaks, vents, reconfigures and hands out trinkets from the Director’s Chair. She lets the party believe she is in the Booth.
  • The finale: She sends her stand-in to the Booth and lets the Doppel-cast do the fighting. When a stand-in falls, she switches to a spare and announces a “second act.”
  • When the Core goes down: She seals the panic room, goes silent, and waits to see who comes looking.
  • When she is found: She talks. She bargains, threatens with the dead-man switch, pleads in the Child’s voice, and offers the Booth to whichever contestant she rated highest. She never attacks; if every other option is gone, she surrenders.
