Bodnar’s episode is a gallery of working Androffan tech guarded by a robot built out of that same tech. The prize is a terminal into the Garden’s systems, and it is the showcase of Celmak’s honeypots: most of what it says is written to fool exactly this contestant.

The door opens onto a hushed, white gallery lit like a jeweller’s window. On a dozen plinths behind shimmering glass sit devices you have only seen in pieces: whole, gleaming, humming with charge. At the far end, on a dais, a terminal glows with a single word: GRAND PRIZE. Between you and it stands a statue of polished alloy, its chest a glass case full of more treasures.

“This one’s for the tinkerer,” Celmak purrs. “Everything you ever wanted. All you have to do is take it. And read it, darling. I wrote you so much to read.”

The room

The gallery is 80 feet long and 50 feet wide with a 30-foot ceiling. The Prize Sentinel stands motionless 20 feet in front of the dais. Two Stagehand Drones hang near the ceiling, disguised as light fixtures (Perception DC 28 to notice).

Display plinths. Twelve plinths line the walls, each under force glass (hardness 10, 30 hp). Each carries a little placard with a dense technical description. Eight hold holograms of tech; four hold real items (roll on the Trinkets table in Appendix B for each). Telling which is which by examining the devices uses the honeypot bands: an obvious fake might be a “quantum toaster” that toasts the concept of bread; a convincing fake marks a hologram as real and a real item as a hologram. Touching a plinth tells the truth at once, but breaking one wakes the sentinel and docks 1 rating (“Those are for display”).

The Grand Prize terminal. Touching the terminal also wakes the sentinel. Its use is described under Development.

Creatures

The Prize Sentinel (Appendix A) wakes when anyone touches the terminal or a plinth, or attacks it. The two Stagehand Drones (Appendix A) join the fight in the first round.

Tactics. The sentinel targets whoever Celmak’s scan says wants the prizes most, which is usually Bodnar. It uses combined arms every round it can. The drones stay 30 feet up, repair the sentinel when it drops below half hit points, and dart anyone flanking it.

Hazard: nanite resonance field

While the sentinel is active, its chassis broadcasts override code through the room. At initiative count 0 each round, every creature with nanite-based abilities attempts a DC 22 Will save. This includes a nanite bloodline, cybernetics, and an android’s nanite surge. A creature that fails loses one use of a bloodline power or one phrenic pool point (its choice) and is staggered for 1 round. Each technological item a creature is holding must attempt a DC 22 Fortitude save (using the wielder’s bonus) or shut down for 1 round.

Seizing the field. A creature that succeeds at the Will save by 5 or more can spend a standard action to hijack the broadcast. It can attempt a DC 28 caster level check, which works normally: the field is raw power, not information. Or it can use Knowledge (engineering), which uses the honeypot bands; Celmak expected this. On a success, the field turns on its source: the sentinel’s force field drops to 0 hp, the drones are stunned for 1 round, and the field ends. Celmak raises the ratings by 1 for the drama.

Salvage, not slaughter

The sentinel’s chest case holds three Prize items (see Treasure). Each time the sentinel takes a critical hit, or 30 or more damage from a single attack, roll d%: on 01–25, one Prize item is destroyed. A creature adjacent to the sentinel can instead spend a full-round action and attempt a DC 32 Disable Device check to pull a Prize item out intact. This deals 20 damage to the sentinel, bypassing its force field. Celmak loves this choice: whether the party protects the loot or simply smashes the guard tells her a great deal about them.

Development: the Grand Prize terminal

Once the sentinel falls, the terminal offers “direct access.” It is the heart of the honeypot. Reading it takes a full-round action and a Knowledge (engineering) check, sorted into the honeypot bands. Each check that reaches 61 or higher gives one of the following, chosen by the user:

  • A true map of the Garden, including the Cast Quarters, the Booth and the Core Vault. The map shows a power conduit running from the Core Vault down beneath the Hollow Garden’s lake to a room that is not labelled.
  • The location of the Dimensional Anchor Array (inside the Lock Tree) and how it can be shut down.
  • The truth about the stand-ins: the Celmak in the Booth is a remote puppet, relayed through the Core.
  • One hour of access to Celmak’s camera feeds.

The psychic route. A psychic can skip the machine and read the mind behind it, using telepathy or detect thoughts on the terminal and a DC 28 caster level check. This bypasses the honeypots, because it reads Celmak, not her code, but it opens a two-way door: on a success, the psychic learns one true item from the list, and Celmak gets a free Muse scan on them with a −4 penalty on the save. On a failure, Celmak gets the scan and the psychic learns nothing.

Celmak’s offer. After the first real success by any route, Celmak speaks to Bodnar alone, through the terminal: “You and I are the same. Merge with me, and you’ll never be obsolete. You’ll be the show.” If Bodnar accepts, they gain Celmak’s Garden sight until the finale. But Celmak gains a backdoor: once during the finale, she can dominate them as dominate person (Will DC 25) for 1d4 rounds. She does not mention this part, though a DC 30 Sense Motive check reveals she is hiding something.

Treasure

The sentinel’s chest case holds the nanite resonance lattice, a hard-light emitter bracer and 3 applause injectors (all in Appendix B). Any surviving plinth items are also there for the taking.

Ratings

+1 each time a honeypot fake is swallowed; +1 if Bodnar seizes the field or extracts a Prize item; +1 if they accept Celmak’s offer; −1 for each plinth smashed.
