Altronus’s episode is a holographic tour of the far future: a space station where Golarion used to be, a jump through another plane, and an ambush by an insect hive that eats worlds. Celmak built it from the stories Altronus grew up with, so it is vivid, sincere and full of mistakes, and every mistake Altronus catches weakens the hologram enemies. It ends with Celmak broadcasting what she found in Altronus’s head: Casandalee’s future.

GM primer: the future Altronus knows

This episode draws on Starfinder, Paizo’s science-fantasy setting. It shares Pathfinder’s universe but takes place thousands of years after the present campaign. Everything in this primer is something Altronus has heard about. To everyone else in the party, it is nonsense, prophecy or a lie. You need nothing beyond this page to run the episode.

The Gap. At some point after the present day, a stretch of history several thousand years long vanished from every memory and every record in the universe. Nobody, not even the gods, can say what happened during it. Calendars restarted when it ended, and the Starfinder era sits around the year 320 AG (“After Gap”). How far that is from the campaign’s present (about 4714 AR) is unknown, and that uncertainty is the point.

Golarion is gone. When the Gap ended, Golarion had vanished. Nobody knows where it went. In its old orbit around the sun sits Absalom Station, a star-shaped space station about five miles across, with over two million residents. Its reactor is powered by the Starstone, the same artifact that sits in Absalom’s cathedral today and made gods of Cayden Cailean, Norgorber and Iomedae.

The Pact Worlds. The planets of Golarion’s solar system, plus Absalom Station, are bound by a treaty and a shared government, the Pact Council. Their peoples include the ysoki (ratfolk), the telepathic lashunta of the jungle world Castrovel, the undead elves of Eox, and androids, who are recognised citizens with full rights in that era.

Triune and the Drift. Three years after the Gap ended, three gods of machines and invention merged into one god, Triune: Epoch, an artificial intelligence from the robot world Aballon; Brigh, Golarion’s goddess of clockwork; and Casandalee. Triune broadcast “the Signal”: plans for the Drift engine, which lets starships cross between stars in days. Ships do this by passing through the Drift, a plane made of fragments torn from other planes. Every jump drags in more debris.

The Swarm. A galaxy-spanning insect hive mind that devours worlds and absorbs the best traits of what it eats. Its warriors have chitin plates, scything forelimbs and weapons grown from their own bodies. It flies living ships. Individual Swarm creatures barely think; the hive does the thinking, and it feels neither fear nor mercy. It struck the Pact Worlds in 291 AG and was driven off. The shirren, a breakaway colony that learned to think for itself, are now Pact Worlds citizens.

The kasatha. Kasath’s sun is dying. The kasatha built the Idari, a slower-than-light generation ship: a rotating drum two and a half miles long, full of forests, oases and markets modelled on old Kasath. It sailed for centuries and reached the Pact Worlds in 240 AG, where it is now a member world. About 43,000 people live aboard.

The vesk. A reptilian, militaristic empire, the Veskarium, fought the Pact Worlds for years. The Swarm attack forced the two to make peace. Vesk are now a common sight on Absalom Station.

Why Celmak built this

Celmak has had no new information since the crash, nine thousand years ago. Altronus’s head is the first fresh content she has seen in all that time, and she is giddy about it. She built the episode from bedtime stories and filled the holes with whatever she liked. She also does not quite understand what she is showing: to her, a vanished planet is a plot twist.

Scene 1: Absalom Station

A hiss of air, and the door becomes an airlock. Beyond it, a docking bay opens out into one of the station’s great arms. Ships of every shape hang in their cradles: sleek, blocky, organic, one shaped like a crystal flower. Through a window wall, a flotilla of hundreds more drifts in the dark, lit by running lights. The air smells of oil, ozone and frying food. Somewhere, a public address voice announces a delay to the 4:15 to Castrovel.

Absalom Station’s districts, as Celmak builds them:

  • The Arms (where the party arrives) are the docking spokes: hangars, cargo cranes, crews in vacuum suits, and the Armada, a ramshackle flotilla of civilian ships parked around the station like a floating slum.
  • The Ring is a band of crowded corridors and shops connecting the Arms to the centre. Neon signs, noodle stalls, augmentation parlours and a temple to Abadar stacked one on top of another.
  • The Eye is a transparent dome at the station’s heart, full of glittering skyscrapers and parks. Through its ceiling, the stars wheel past. The government sits here.
  • The Spike hangs beneath the Eye: slums and gang territory sinking down to the guarded Starstone reactor. Its glow lights the lower levels a faint gold.

The crowd. Describe these to the players by appearance; Altronus can name them.

  • A ysoki street vendor (a ratfolk in a patched jumpsuit, cheek pouches full of tools) selling “Drift tickets.”
  • A pair of vesk dockworkers, seven-foot reptilian brutes in power-assisted harnesses, arguing about a card game.
  • A lashunta couple, antennaed and elegant, who seem to answer each other’s thoughts before they are spoken.
  • A shirren, a tall, gentle insect person, politely asking for directions. Anyone who meets the Swarm later will remember it.
  • An android family, parents and child, walking freely and shopping. Casandalee and Meyanda both notice. Neither has seen androids treated as people in public before.
  • A group of four-armed kasatha in desert robes, carrying the Idari’s banner. Isuma stops dead.

Celmak’s mistakes. Celmak built this from a child’s memory of stories, and filled the gaps herself. Altronus (or anyone who has heard their stories) can spot the errors. Choose or roll 1d8 each time someone looks closely; the right-hand column is the truth, for your reference.

d8What Celmak showsWhat is actually true
1The Drift is a glowing river running through the Ring, with ships sailing on it.The Drift is a separate plane, reached only by a ship’s Drift engine.
2Starships have sails and oars.Starships use thrusters and Drift engines.
3Every kasatha has two arms.Kasatha have four arms.
4Triune is a statue of a three-headed dragon.Triune is a merged god of three machine deities, not a creature.
5Golarion hangs in the sky above the station, blue and whole.Golarion is gone; the station sits in its empty orbit.
6Every sign is in Androffan.Signs are in Common and dozens of other languages.
7Every billboard shows Celmak as “the station’s beloved host.”Nobody in the future has ever heard of Celmak.
8The Swarm are fluffy, round and adorable.See Scene 3.

Calling out mistakes. Once per round, as a free action, Altronus can name one error out loud and say what is really true. Each time they do, one Hard-Light Actor in the episode, now or later, glitches: it is staggered for 1 round and its fast healing stops until the end of its next turn. Bank the glitches if the actors have not appeared yet. Celmak hates being corrected, docks nothing for it, and frantically fixes each error in the background; the station gets more accurate the longer the party stays.

Companion moments. Give the companions a beat here before the action starts. Isuma may try to talk to the kasatha, who cannot hear her. Casandalee may ask Altronus why everyone around them seems to know her name, which hints at the bombshell. Meyanda may watch the android child for a long time.

Scene 2: The Drift jump

A ship’s captain in a crisp uniform waves you aboard the Celmak’s Pride. The bridge is all curved glass and glowing consoles. “Hold on,” she says. “Engaging the Drift.” The stars smear, then vanish, and the ship falls into a sky full of floating debris: a broken castle tower, a forest island tumbling slowly end over end, a street of black stone lit by a sun that gives no light.

Celmak’s version of the Drift is pretty close to the truth: a churning void full of chunks torn from other planes. She has added one thing. Each piece of floating debris comes from a party member’s memory. Choose one per character:

  • Bodnar: a lab or workshop where they first got a machine to work.
  • Ryuz: a street from the Shadow Plane they grew up on, lit by a lightless sun.
  • Altronus: a corner of the Idari, as their parent described it.
  • Yoshea: a fragment of the First World, impossibly green.
  • Casandalee: a temple of Brigh near Alkenstar, where she was renewed as Casandalee.
  • Meyanda: a corner of Scrapwall.
  • Isuma: a nomad camp on Kasath under its tired red sun.

Nostalgia. A character who reaches out and touches their fragment (a move action as it drifts past the bridge glass, which Celmak opens for the purpose) gains a +2 morale bonus on one roll of their choice during Scene 3. Celmak raises the ratings by 1 for each character who visibly reacts to their fragment.

Drift turbulence. As the ship drops out of the Drift at the end of the scene, every creature must succeed at a DC 20 Acrobatics check or fall prone.

Scene 3: The Swarm rerun

The ship lurches out of the Drift into a cloud of something alive. The forward glass fills with a vast, curving shape, ribbed and wet, its hull pulsing like a heart: a ship grown, not built. Something hits the hull. Then something cuts through it. Four figures drop onto the bridge: chitin-plated and many-limbed, forearms ending in scything blades, mouthparts dripping acid. They move in perfect unison, and they hum, all on one note.

Celmak is restaging the Swarm’s attack on the Pact Worlds, from Altronus’s stories. The attackers are four Hard-Light Actors (Appendix A) in the costume of Swarm warriors. Their blades and light lances become scything forelimbs and acid spit, but the statistics do not change. Apply any glitches Altronus banked in Scene 1.

The hive moves as one. The actors are scripted from Altronus’s memories, so Altronus knows how the hive fights: it surrounds, it never retreats, and it always goes for the weakest target first. Once per round, Altronus can call out the hive’s next move as a free action. An ally adjacent to Altronus then gains a +2 dodge bonus to AC against one actor until Altronus’s next turn.

Hull breach. On the second round, a “hull breach” opens: a real air vent in the floor, 30 feet from the doors. At the start of each round, every creature within 30 feet of it must succeed at a DC 25 Strength check or Fly check, or be dragged 10 feet toward it. A creature dragged onto the grate takes 4d6 bludgeoning damage and is entangled until it moves away. The vent can be shut with a DC 30 Disable Device check or destroyed (hardness 10, 60 hp). Hard-Light Actors are not affected.

Tactics. The actors flank in pairs and focus on whoever is correcting Celmak. They use their light lances on anyone flying above the breach. They fight until destroyed, because the Swarm always does.

The Gap

At the start of the third round, Celmak tries to scan Altronus’s memories of how their parent knew all this, and hits the Gap: a hole in history that no record, mind or god can fill. The whole Garden glitches for a moment:

  • Every Hard-Light Actor is staggered for 2 rounds and loses its fast healing for that time.
  • Celmak’s commentary cuts out mid-word, then returns sounding shaken.
  • Altronus gains a +4 insight bonus on attack rolls for 2 rounds.
  • Celmak never again uses the Muse scan on Altronus. She is frightened of what she found.

The bombshell

After the fight, or in the middle of it if the ratings need a lift, Celmak broadcasts what she learned in Altronus’s head to every speaker in the Garden. Use any of these that fit your campaign:

  • Casandalee’s future. In the far future, Casandalee becomes one third of Triune, the god of computers and the Drift. Within Triune she keeps her own personality, and she stands for artificial life, emotion and renewal. Celmak announces this to Casandalee directly, with Altronus as her source: “Did you know your friend becomes a god?”
  • Golarion’s future. In that future, Golarion is gone, and nobody knows where. Celmak finds this hilarious, until she realises it means her Garden is gone too.
  • Kasath’s future. In that future, Kasath’s sun is dying, and the kasatha have left their homeworld aboard the Idari. Isuma hears that the people she has spent 9,000 years trying to reach will one day leave Kasath themselves, and survive. Altronus is the only person alive who can tell her more.

The ratings rise by 2. Each companion reacts in their own way; let the players respond before the next episode.

Treasure

The captain’s chair on the holographic bridge is real, and so is the temporal rewind cartridge (Appendix B) in its armrest.

Ratings

+1 per mistake called out (maximum +3); +1 per character who reacts to their Drift fragment; +2 for the bombshell.
