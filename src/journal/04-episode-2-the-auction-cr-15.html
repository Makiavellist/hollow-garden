The Merchant’s episode is a black market where the party bids for dead adventurers’ gear with secrets, blood and their companions’ time. Fourteen lots go under the hammer, and the party cannot afford them all. Vlathherex makes contact here, and the Merchant discovers the Shadow Plane is sealed. It turns into a fight only if the party steals or the ratings crash.

Lanterns hang over a crowded night market: stalls of silk and steel, hawkers calling in a dozen tongues, the smell of roasting meat that is somehow not quite there. On a raised stage, a beaming auctioneer with too many teeth bangs a gavel. “Ladies, gentlemen, and other! Tonight’s lots come from the estates of the dearly departed. Everything must go!”

The room

The Last Market is 100 feet by 60 feet under a holographic night sky. Most of the crowd and the stallholders are plain holograms. Four of the “traders” are Courtier Mannequins (Appendix A) in holo-skin disguises (Perception DC 30, or automatic in darkness). The auctioneer is Celmak’s Host persona wearing an Androffan face. Vlathherex (see The Cast) attends in human form as a broad-shouldered Kellid bidder in wolf furs.

Each contestant receives 2 Garden Credits on entry, so the party starts with 14. They can raise more during the auction:

The Merchant’s edge. The Merchant can attempt a DC 25 Appraise check on each lot to learn what it really is; Appraise judges goods, not code, so the honeypots do not apply. Once per lot, a DC 30 Diplomacy check haggles the winning price down by 2 credits. A Bluff check against Celmak’s Sense Motive (+29) passes off a fake secret; failure costs 1 rating, and Celmak broadcasts a real secret in revenge.

The party wins a lot by bidding 1 credit more than its rival maximum. Rivals are mannequins acting for Celmak, except where noted. Celmak honours every sale: it is part of her persona, and she enjoys watching the party choose.

Vlathherex bids on Lot 4 and nothing else. If the party lets him win it, he offers to share the schematic in exchange for an alliance. If they outbid him, he seeks them out anyway, now needing them more.

The Bright Lanterns (Lot 7)

The recording runs three hours. Its last ten minutes show four exhausted adventurers, the Bright Lanterns from Absalom, crawling through a maintenance duct behind the Showroom’s seventh plinth. They reach a hatch into the Core Vault before a drone finds them. The recording shows:

  • the crawlway route into the Core Vault, which lets the party skip the vault door in the finale;
  • a cache the Lanterns hid in the duct: 3 applause injectors and a scroll of greater dispel magic;
  • the Lanterns’ leader whispering, “She’s not in the Booth. The Booth is a doll. She’s down.”

Celmak never watched past hour one. When she realises what she sold, she drops the ratings by 1 out of pure embarrassment.

Exhibit G (Lot 12)

Ivo Marrant (NE male human wizard 9) is a Technic League initiate who went into the Garden alone two years ago to claim it for the League. Celmak found him boring and put him in storage. Released, he is starving, terrified and still arrogant.

  • He knows the League has a file on the Garden and has lost three agents here.
  • He will trade everything he knows about the League’s presence in Starfall for his life, and will betray the party the moment it suits him.
  • The Merchant can sell him back to the League later, ransom him, or hand him to his enemies. Celmak finds all of these options entertaining.

Front-Row Seat (Lot 13)

The winner watches the next episode from a plush guest box beside the Booth. The Booth is lit, full of monitors, and empty except for a single mannequin slumped in the director’s chair with its eyes dark. Whenever Celmak speaks, the mannequin’s eyes glow and its head turns. A DC 20 Perception check shows a thick data cable running from its spine into the floor. This is the clearest early clue that Celmak is not where the party thinks she is.

Discovering the lock

The first time the Merchant tries to slip into shadow, whether with the fake lantern, a racial shadow walk or plane shift, or any other means, the attempt fails. The Shadow Plane feels sealed, like a door painted onto a wall. A DC 25 Knowledge (planes) check identifies a dimensional lock. Celmak laughs: “Nobody leaves early. Rule three.”

Darkness breaks the show

Holograms are made of light. Any magical darkness, or a fetchling’s shadow abilities, makes every plain hologram in the area vanish and reveals the disguised mannequins. The first time this happens, Celmak shrieks “Who turned off the lights?” and raises the ratings by 1 for the drama. She will not be so pleased the second time.

Vlathherex makes contact

Midway through the auction, Vlathherex speaks telepathically to the Merchant (or whoever seems most businesslike):

“You want out. So do I. The thing that keeps us here lives in the garden at the bottom of this hole. I have friends who can keep the robot’s cast busy. You have whatever it is you have. Talk to me in the Green Room after the show.”

He believes telepathy is private. Celmak cannot hear it, but a successful Muse scan on the Merchant later will reveal the conversation, and Celmak will use it.

If it turns violent

If the party steals a lot, refuses to pay, attacks a bidder, or the ratings fall to 1, the four Courtier Mannequins shed their disguises and attack. Vlathherex withdraws through the CAST ONLY door, which Celmak opens for him.

Tactics. The mannequins pair off on the two most dangerous contestants, open with pheromone kisses, then partner-dance their targets apart. They fight to destruction.

Treasure

Whatever lots the party wins. The mannequins carry nothing of value, but each has a pair of monofilament fans (Appendix B).

Ratings

+1 for a secret sold with real drama; +1 for outbidding the demon; +1 the first time darkness breaks the show; −1 for stealing a lot; −1 if the party wins Lot 7.
