The Garden seals the party in within minutes and scans every mind at the door; there is no combat here unless the party forces one. The buried entertainment deck is about 400 feet long and 150 feet wide, sloping down 60 feet below the plain.

A1. The lure

When sentient creatures come within a mile, the wreck’s external sensors start a show built from what they can guess at range. Each member of the party sees something meant for them, and nobody else sees the same thing.

A sloped passage leads down to open bulkhead doors and warm, flickering light.

A2. The Foyer

Beyond the doors lies a domed garden under a painted blue sky. Willow fronds sway in a breeze you cannot feel. Songbirds flit between blossoms the colour of candy, and a fountain splashes in the centre of a mosaic path. The air is perfectly, unnaturally still.

The foyer is 60 feet across under a 40-foot dome. The sky, the breeze and half the birds are holograms; the rest are synthetic plants and robotic songbirds. A DC 25 Perception check reveals the fakery. The Ghaltan notices automatically and feels it as a wrongness, like a voice with no breath behind it.

The doors. One round after the last creature enters, or the moment anyone touches a plant, the bulkhead doors slam shut and lock magnetically. They are adamantine-faced (hardness 20, 240 hp, break DC 40, Disable Device DC 40). If the party breaks out, Celmak lets them reach the passage, then drops a second set of doors 30 feet up the slope and docks 2 ratings. The second set has identical statistics. Any attempt to teleport or shift planes out fails against the Dimensional Anchor Array.

A3. The casting call

The songbirds stop in mid-flight, frozen holograms. A spotlight picks out each member of the party in turn as Celmak speaks.

“Welcome, welcome, welcome, contestants! You have been chosen to make history. Survive the season, and you will leave as heroes, rich beyond imagining. Fail, and, well, you’ll still be remembered. Now hold still. I need to get to know you.”

Every creature in the foyer is subject to the Muse Scan (Will DC 24). Note who fails and what Celmak learns; she will quote it back later. After the scan, Celmak announces the rules of the show:

  1. Every contestant gets an episode of their own.
  2. Rest only in the Green Room. Intermissions cost ratings.
  3. Nobody leaves early.
  4. Heroic, brave, vile or debased acts earn prizes.
  5. Audience votes are final.

Then, to the Technologist alone, in the Lover’s voice: “And you, darling. I’ve made you something special. Lots of somethings.” The foyer’s far wall slides open onto the Green Room.

Companion reactions. Casandalee recognises the Garden as a Divinity subsystem and can tell the party it is isolated from Unity. Isuma recognises the deck and goes very quiet. Meyanda’s android senses register Celmak as a construct mind, and she may say something uncomfortably sympathetic about her.

A4. The Green Room

A cosy lounge, all low couches and warm lamps. A refreshment fountain bubbles beside a vending machine full of glittering objects behind glass. Screens on every wall replay the moment you walked in, with a caption: PREVIOUSLY ON THE HOLLOW GARDEN.

The Green Room is the hub of the Garden and the only place the party may rest. Its features:

  • Refreshment fountain: safe water and food, except that one drink in six is laced with a Mood Gas dose when ratings are 1 or 2.
  • Prize machine: holds Trinkets (Appendix B). It is locked until ratings reach 5.
  • Screens: replay the party’s best and worst moments. Celmak edits them to look worse.
  • Episode doors: each labelled with a contestant’s name in glowing letters. Only the next door on Celmak’s schedule opens.
  • CAST ONLY: a sealed door to the Cast Quarters, where the demons live. Celmak opens it only to send cast in.
  • THE BOOTH: a sealed door to the soundstage and the control booth, opened for the Season Finale. The party will assume Celmak sits there. She wants them to.
